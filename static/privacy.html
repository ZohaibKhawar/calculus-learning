Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 3, 2026

CalcLearners ("we", "us") is a free calculus study site operated from Ontario, Canada. The site's operator is responsible for the personal information it holds. This policy explains what information the site keeps about you, why, and what you can do about it. It goes with our Terms and Conditions and Cookie Policy.

The short version

  • There are no accounts. You never give us an email address, a password, your name, a phone number or payment details.
  • Your browser gets a random ID in a cookie, and your progress and posts are saved against that ID.
  • There are no ads and no analytics trackers, and we never sell your information.
  • A few outside services help run the site (hosting, fonts, code libraries and optional AI lessons). They are listed in section 3.

1. What we collect

  • A visitor ID. The first time your browser loads the site, the server gives it a random ID and keeps it in a cookie. The ID is not linked to your name or to an account anywhere else.
  • Display name. Optional. It is the name shown on your forum posts ("Anonymous learner" if you leave it empty), and you can change it at any time.
  • Study activity. Lessons you mark as understood, quiz scores, notes you write, the last lesson you opened, the days you studied (for your streak) and your progress through video lessons.
  • Forum activity. Your questions, answers, upvotes, "helpful" marks and any reports you make about other posts. Everyone who visits the site can see your posts together with your display name.
  • Feedback and email. Anything you send through the Send feedback page. If you email us, we receive your email address and your message. They stay in our mailbox, which is provided by Google, and are not connected to your visitor ID.
  • Uploads. If you upload notes (PDF, Word or text), we read the file's text to find matching lessons, and we keep the file name and the topics it matched. The file itself is deleted straight away unless it is a PDF being turned into an AI video lesson. In that case we keep the file and the lesson made from it.
  • Technical information. Your IP address is used to limit abuse, such as too many posts or too many new visitors from one network. Those counters are normally cleared within a few days. Our hosting provider also keeps standard server logs (IP address, browser type and the pages requested).
  • Cookies and browser storage. One essential cookie that holds your visitor ID, and a few saved preferences. See the Cookie Policy.

Until October 2026 the site had sign-in. The usernames, email addresses, passwords and Google account IDs from that time have been deleted.

2. Why we use it

  • to remember your progress, notes and scores and show them back to you;
  • to run the Q&A forum and show posts to other learners;
  • to build study plans and video lessons from files you upload;
  • to keep the site safe: limits on repeated requests, moderation, and blocking browsers that are used to break the Terms;
  • to read feedback and improve the site.

We don't use your information for advertising. The only automated checks are the forum's word filter and the abuse limits described above.

By using the site you consent to these uses. You can withdraw that consent by deleting your data (section 6), but the site can't save anything for you without this information.

3. Who we share it with

We don't sell or rent personal information. These service providers help run the site, and each handles information under its own privacy policy:

  • PythonAnywhere (hosting). The site and its database run on PythonAnywhere's servers.
  • Google Fonts (the site's typefaces, loaded on every page). Google receives your IP address and browser details when your browser downloads them.
  • jsDelivr (delivers the math and animation libraries). It receives your IP address when your browser downloads them.
  • Anthropic (AI lessons). When AI lessons are switched on, a PDF you upload is sent to Anthropic's Claude service to build the lesson. Please don't upload files that contain personal information about you or anyone else.

Other visitors see your display name and forum posts. We may also disclose information when the law requires it, or when it is needed to protect someone's safety or the security of the site. If someone else takes over running CalcLearners, your information would move to them under this policy, and we would say so on the home page first.

4. Where your information is kept

Our service providers store and process information outside Canada, including in the United States. While it is there it is subject to that country's laws, and its courts and authorities may be able to access it.

5. How long we keep it

  • Progress, notes, uploads and feedback: until you delete them, use "Delete my data", or your browser has gone 12 months without a visit.
  • Forum posts: until you or a moderator delete them. After 12 months without a visit from your browser they stay up, but are no longer linked to any visitor ID.
  • Emails: until we have dealt with them, or until you ask us to delete them.
  • Abuse-limit counters: normally a few days.
  • The visitor ID cookie: up to 12 months after your last visit.
  • Blocked browsers: if we block a browser for breaking the Terms, we keep a minimal record so the block keeps working.

If your cookie is cleared or expires, what was saved under that ID is no longer connected to your browser. It is deleted 12 months after that browser's last visit.

6. Your choices and rights

On the My progress page you can change your display name, use "Reset my progress" to clear your progress, quiz scores and notes, or use "Delete my data" to delete everything saved for your browser. "Delete my data" asks you to type a confirmation, happens straight away and can't be undone: your progress, quiz scores, notes, uploads and the lessons made from them, feedback, votes and forum posts go, along with the answers other learners posted under your questions. A lesson made from a file that another learner also uploaded stays available to them. You can also delete individual forum posts of your own.

You can ask us for a copy of the information we hold about you, or to correct it, through the Send feedback page or by emailing CalcLearners@gmail.com. We aim to answer within 30 days.

Because there are no accounts, the cookie in your browser is the only link between you and your data. If you ask about data from a browser whose cookie is gone, we will ask for details that help us find it, such as your display name and what you posted. If we can't be reasonably sure the data is yours, we may not be able to act on the request.

If you are not satisfied with how we handle your information, you can contact the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada or the privacy regulator in your province or country.

7. Security

The site is served over HTTPS, the visitor ID cookie is signed and can't be read by page scripts, and repeated requests are limited. No website can promise perfect security. If a breach creates a real risk of significant harm, we will post a notice on the site and report it as the law requires.

8. Children and teens

CalcLearners is written for high-school and college calculus students, and everything on it is math. It is meant for learners aged 13 and up. That is about privacy, not content: the forum is public, and younger children can't agree for themselves to how their information is handled. If you are under 13, use the site with a parent or guardian and ask them before you post. If you are under the age of majority where you live, get a parent's or guardian's permission first. If you believe a child under 13 has posted personal details, or is using the site without a parent's involvement, tell us and we will delete what we can identify as theirs.

9. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy we will update the date at the top. If a change is significant, we will also say so on the home page before it takes effect.

10. Contact

The site's operator is the person responsible for privacy at CalcLearners. Email CalcLearners@gmail.com with questions, requests or complaints, or use the "Anything else?" box on the Send feedback page.
