Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 5, 2026

CalcLearners ("we", "us") is a free calculus study site operated from Ontario, Canada. The site's operator is responsible for the personal information it holds. This policy explains what information the site keeps about you, why, and what you can do about it. It goes with our Terms and Conditions and Cookie Policy.

The short version

  • You don't need an account. If you choose to create one, it is a username, a password and a recovery code, and nothing else. We never ask for an email address, your name, a phone number or payment details.
  • Your browser gets a random ID in a cookie, and your progress and posts are saved against that ID. An account lets you reach them from another browser.
  • There are no ads on the site and no analytics trackers, and we never sell your information.
  • A few outside services help run the site (hosting, fonts, code libraries and optional AI features). They are listed in section 3.

1. What we collect

  • A visitor ID. The first time your browser loads the site, the server gives it a random ID and keeps it in a cookie. The ID is not linked to your name or to an account anywhere else.
  • An account, if you create one. A username you choose, a password, and a recovery code that the site makes for you. We keep the password and the recovery code only in a scrambled (hashed) form that can't be turned back into either. The username is used only for signing in and is not shown to other visitors; please don't use your real name or an email address for it. We don't ask for an email address, so a forgotten password can only be reset with the recovery code.
  • Display name. Optional. It is the name shown on your forum posts ("Anonymous learner" if you leave it empty), and you can change it at any time.
  • Study activity. Lessons you mark as understood, quiz scores, notes you write, the last lesson you opened, the days you studied (for your streak) and your progress through video lessons.
  • How you found us. If you arrive by clicking one of our ads, we note which ad service it came from (for example "google") against your visitor ID. We receive nothing else about you from the ad service, and we send nothing about your visit back to it.
  • Forum activity. Your questions, replies, likes and dislikes, and any reports you make about other posts. Everyone who visits the site can see your posts together with your display name.
  • Chat with CalcBot. What you type to CalcBot, the study helper, is sent to our server to get a reply, along with which lesson you have open. We don't save it. The conversation is only on the page in front of you, and is gone when you close the chat, press "Start a new chat" or leave the page: opening the chat again starts a new one. If you attach a picture, your browser first shrinks it and leaves out the details a photo file carries, such as where it was taken. The picture is then sent to our server with your message. We don't save it, and your browser forgets it when you leave or reload the page.
  • Feedback and email. Anything you send through the Send feedback page. If you email us, we receive your email address and your message. They stay in our mailbox, which is provided by Google, and are not connected to your visitor ID.
  • Uploads. If you upload notes (PDF, Word or text), we read the file's text to find matching lessons, and we keep the file name and the topics it matched. The file itself is deleted straight away unless it is a PDF being turned into an AI video lesson. In that case we keep the file and the lesson made from it.
  • Technical information. Your IP address is used to limit abuse, such as too many posts, too many new visitors from one network, or too many tries at signing in or at resetting a password (those counters also hold the username that was tried). The counters are normally cleared within a few days. Our hosting provider also keeps standard server logs (IP address, browser type and the pages requested).
  • Cookies and browser storage. One essential cookie that holds your visitor ID and keeps you signed in if you have an account, and a few saved preferences. See the Cookie Policy.

An earlier version of the site had a different sign-in until the start of October 2026. The usernames, email addresses, passwords and Google account IDs from that version have been deleted, so accounts from then no longer exist.

2. Why we use it

  • to remember your progress, notes and scores and show them back to you;
  • to sign you in to your account, if you have one, and to check it is you before its username, password or recovery code is changed or the account is deleted;
  • to run the Q&A forum and show posts to other learners;
  • to build study plans and video lessons from files you upload;
  • to keep the site safe: limits on repeated requests, moderation, and blocking browsers and accounts that are used to break the Terms;
  • to see whether our ads reach learners who find the site useful;
  • to read feedback and improve the site.

We don't use your information to target ads, and we don't share it with advertisers. The only automated checks are the check on new forum posts and the abuse limits described above. The forum check scores whether a post fits the forum, using built-in rules and, when AI features are switched on, an AI service (section 3). A post that scores too low isn't posted, and you are told why straight away so you can reword it.

By using the site you consent to these uses. You can withdraw that consent by deleting your data (section 6), but the site can't save anything for you without this information.

3. Who we share it with

We don't sell or rent personal information. These service providers help run the site, and each handles information under its own privacy policy:

  • PythonAnywhere (hosting). The site and its database run on PythonAnywhere's servers.
  • Google Fonts (the site's typefaces, loaded on every page). Google receives your IP address and browser details when your browser downloads them.
  • jsDelivr (delivers the math and animation libraries). It receives your IP address when your browser downloads them.
  • Anthropic (AI features). When they are switched on, three things are sent to Anthropic's Claude service: a PDF you upload, to build a video lesson; what you type to CalcBot and any picture you attach, to write its reply; and the text of a new forum post, to check that it fits the forum. Your visitor ID and display name are not sent with them. Please don't put personal information about you or anyone else in uploads, chat messages, pictures or posts.

Other visitors see your display name and forum posts. We may also disclose information when the law requires it, or when it is needed to protect someone's safety or the security of the site. If someone else takes over running CalcLearners, your information would move to them under this policy, and we would say so on the home page first.

4. Where your information is kept

Our service providers store and process information outside Canada, including in the United States. While it is there it is subject to that country's laws, and its courts and authorities may be able to access it.

5. How long we keep it

  • Progress, notes, uploads and feedback: until you delete them, use "Delete my data" (or "Delete my account"), or your browser or account has gone 12 months without a visit.
  • Accounts: until you delete the account, or until it has gone 12 months without being used. The username and the scrambled password and recovery code are deleted with it.
  • Forum posts: until you or a moderator delete them. After 12 months without a visit from your browser or account they stay up, but are no longer linked to any visitor ID.
  • Chat with CalcBot: not kept on our server. Your browser drops the conversation, and any pictures in it, when you close the chat. Pictures you attach are not kept on our server either.
  • Emails: until we have dealt with them, or until you ask us to delete them.
  • Abuse-limit counters: normally a few days.
  • The visitor ID cookie: up to 12 months after your last visit.
  • Blocked browsers and accounts: if we block a browser or an account for breaking the Terms, we keep a minimal record so the block keeps working.

If your cookie is cleared or expires, what was saved under that ID is no longer connected to your browser. With an account you can sign in again to reach it. Without one, it is deleted 12 months after that browser's last visit.

6. Your choices and rights

On the My progress page you can change your display name, use "Reset my progress" to clear your progress, quiz scores and notes, or use "Delete my data" to delete everything saved for your browser. "Delete my data" asks you to type a confirmation, happens straight away and can't be undone: your progress, quiz scores, notes, uploads and the lessons made from them, feedback, votes and forum posts go, along with the answers other learners posted under your questions. A lesson made from a file that another learner also uploaded stays available to them. You can also delete individual forum posts of your own.

If you have an account, your profile page shows what the account holds. There you can change your username or password, get a new recovery code, and sign out of every browser. The delete button there is called "Delete my account": it deletes the account together with everything saved in it. Changing the username, password or recovery code and deleting the account each ask for your current password first, instead of a typed confirmation.

When you sign in on a browser that has saved something on its own, you choose whether to move it into your account. If you don't, it stays with that browser and is there again when you sign out. If you forget your password, "Forgot your password?" on the sign-in page resets it with your recovery code; the code works once, and you are given a new one.

You can ask us for a copy of the information we hold about you, or to correct it, through the Send feedback page or by emailing CalcLearners@gmail.com. We aim to answer within 30 days.

Without an account, the cookie in your browser is the only link between you and your data. If you ask about data from a browser whose cookie is gone, or about an account you can no longer sign in to, we will ask for details that help us find it, such as your username or display name and what you posted. If we can't be reasonably sure the data is yours, we may not be able to act on the request.

If you are not satisfied with how we handle your information, you can contact the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada or the privacy regulator in your province or country.

7. Security

The site is served over HTTPS, the visitor ID cookie is signed and can't be read by page scripts, passwords and recovery codes are kept only in a salted, scrambled form, and repeated requests, including tries at signing in or resetting a password, are limited. No website can promise perfect security. If a breach creates a real risk of significant harm, we will post a notice on the site and report it as the law requires.

8. Children and teens

CalcLearners is written for high-school and college calculus students, and everything on it is math. It is meant for learners aged 13 and up. That is about privacy, not content: the forum is public, and younger children can't agree for themselves to how their information is handled. If you are under 13, use the site with a parent or guardian and ask them before you post. If you are under the age of majority where you live, get a parent's or guardian's permission first. If you believe a child under 13 has posted personal details, or is using the site without a parent's involvement, tell us and we will delete what we can identify as theirs.

9. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy we will update the date at the top. If a change is significant, we will also say so on the home page before it takes effect.

10. Contact

The site's operator is the person responsible for privacy at CalcLearners. Email CalcLearners@gmail.com with questions, requests or complaints, or use the "Anything else?" box on the Send feedback page.
